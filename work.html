07 / Smart Cities
Cornell Tech · Roosevelt Island
Autonomous mobility

LumaPod: energy by day, mobility by night.

The idea. A dual-purpose fleet of six to eight autonomous electric pods designed for Cornell Tech and Roosevelt Island. By day, rooftop solar panels collect energy for storage and potential support of campus pathway lighting, pickup zones, and emergency infrastructure. By night, the same three-passenger pods become an on-demand ride concept connecting campus, residential buildings, and tram and subway stations.

The problem. Students travel between Cornell Tech, housing, and transit late at night, when existing transportation can be inconsistent. LumaPod explores how renewable energy infrastructure and nighttime mobility could work together to improve safety and convenience.

Service concept. Proposed nighttime service from 8 PM to 3 AM, with app-based ride requests, real-time tracking, designated illuminated pickup points, and pedestrian-priority low-speed operation.

Design considerations. The concept addresses accessible step-free entry, emergency communication, minimal ride-data collection, and pickup/drop-off zones informed by NACTO's Blueprint for Autonomous Urbanism.

My process. Used Gemini AI to iterate concept visualizations, Apple Photos tools to refine the renderings, and Figma to design a mobile app concept for ride requests, pickup selection, pod tracking, and solar-energy status.

Concept proposal — not a deployed autonomous transportation service.

08 / Urban Systems
Manhattan District 9
Fall 2026

Manhattan District 9: Urban Systems.

Project status. Ongoing study of hard and soft urban systems in Manhattan District 9. The current published ArcGIS StoryMap documents our hard-systems investigation; our soft-systems research is still in progress.

Hard systems / Phase 1. New York's transportation systems are built for everyone—but can everyone actually use them? Through a Saturday evening field visit along Broadway, we investigated the physical infrastructure connecting Morningside Heights, Manhattanville, and Hamilton Heights.

The finding. Our analysis identified sixteen entrances to the 1 train across the district, but only one step-free entrance, serving the uptown platform at 137th Street–City College. Stairs, the Manhattanville valley, and steep transfer routes expose the difference between being connected on a map and being able to travel independently.

The approach. Combined field observations and footage with MTA subway station, entrance, bus-stop, and GTFS-realtime data, plus American Community Survey age estimates. ArcGIS StoryMaps brought together live train positions, bus accessibility, geographic context, and a swipe comparison of older residents and step-free subway access.

The takeaway. Accessibility is a property of the whole journey—not simply a station or a transit line. The story makes visible the barriers hidden inside a seemingly well-connected network.

Hard-systems phase documented in ArcGIS StoryMaps · Soft-systems phase in progress.
